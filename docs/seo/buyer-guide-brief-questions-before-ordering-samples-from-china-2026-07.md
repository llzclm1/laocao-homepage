# Buyer Guide Brief: Questions Before Ordering Samples From China

This brief defines one future Buyer Guide page for overseas buyers who want to clarify sample scope before spending time or money with a Chinese supplier.

## Target URL

/buyer-guides/questions-before-ordering-samples-from-china/

## Target Keyword

questions before ordering samples from China

## Buyer Intent

The buyer is not only asking how to order a sample. They are trying to avoid paying for a sample that cannot answer the real business question: whether the supplier understands the product, customization, packaging, timeline, and next-step terms well enough to continue the sourcing conversation.

They may also be worried that a sample fee, fast reply, or attractive quoted price creates false confidence before important details are written down.

## Direct Answer

Before ordering samples from China, ask the supplier what the sample will prove, what it will not prove, and which details will still need confirmation before bulk production. The most useful questions cover product specification, customization scope, packaging, sample lead time, shipping method, sample fee, refund policy, and whether the sample matches the future bulk order.

This is a communication clarity step, not proof that the supplier is verified, audited, safe, or reliable.

## Quick Checklist

- Confirm the exact product model, material, size, color, and finish.
- Ask whether the sample is stock, customized, pre-production, or hand-made.
- Confirm which logo, label, packaging, or accessory details are included.
- Ask what the sample does not include compared with bulk production.
- Confirm sample fee, mold fee, printing fee, and any refundable amount.
- Confirm sample production time and whether delays are common.
- Confirm shipping method, shipping cost, delivery estimate, and who pays.
- Ask whether the sample photos or videos can be shared before shipment.
- Ask whether the bulk order will use the same material, process, supplier, and production line.
- Confirm what changes are allowed after sample review.
- Ask what written approvals the supplier needs before mass production.
- Confirm the next quotation basis after the sample is reviewed.

## Page Outline

### H1

Questions Before Ordering Samples From China

### Intro

Open with the common buyer problem: a supplier says "yes, we can send sample," but the buyer still does not know whether the sample will answer the right questions.

### What this does and does not mean

Explain that sample questions help clarify communication, scope, and assumptions. They do not verify the supplier, audit the factory, guarantee quality, or make payment safe.

### Communication signals

- Supplier answers specific questions instead of only saying "yes."
- Supplier separates stock sample, custom sample, and pre-production sample.
- Supplier explains fees and lead time before asking for payment.
- Supplier can describe what will still need confirmation after the sample.

### What is still unclear

- Whether bulk production will match the sample.
- Whether the supplier owns the process or outsources part of it.
- Whether packaging, labeling, and compliance details are included.
- Whether price changes after sample review.
- Whether payment account details are consistent later.

### Questions to ask supplier

Group questions by product, customization, packaging, timeline, payment, shipment, and next step.

### Suggested message to supplier

Provide a concise buyer message that asks for sample scope, sample fee, lead time, shipping cost, what is included, what is excluded, and what needs approval before bulk order.

### When to slow down

List signals such as vague answers, pressure to pay quickly, changing fee details, unclear sample type, refusal to confirm specifications in writing, or inconsistent payment/account information.

### Boundary note

State the limits clearly.

### FAQ

Answer common sample-order questions without promising supplier safety or quality outcomes.

## Risks

- Avoid saying that sample questions verify a supplier.
- Avoid implying that a good sample proves factory reliability.
- Avoid presenting sample review as a factory audit or inspection.
- Avoid saying that clear sample terms make payment safe.
- Avoid ranking suppliers as safe, reliable, real, or guaranteed.
- Avoid telling buyers to proceed based only on photos, videos, or fast replies.
