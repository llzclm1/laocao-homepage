## 1. LinkedIn Long Post

Before ordering samples from a Chinese supplier, price and shipping cost are not the only questions that matter.

The better question is:

What is this sample supposed to prove?

A sample can help you check product direction, material, size, color, finish, packaging, or customization.

But if the purpose is not clear, buyer and supplier may both say "sample" while meaning different things.

Before paying for a sample, I would confirm five points:

1. Sample purpose
Is this sample meant to check appearance, material, function, packaging, logo, size, or production feasibility?

2. Exact version
Is the supplier sending a stock sample, a similar sample, a modified sample, or a fully custom sample?

3. Product details
Are material, size, color, finish, logo position, and any customization written clearly?

4. Packaging and shipping
Is packaging the same as the future bulk order, or only temporary sample packaging? What shipping method will be used?

5. Bulk differences
What may change before mass production: material batch, mold, packaging, color, lead time, MOQ, or unit price?

A sample is useful when it answers a specific question.

It is risky when both sides treat it as proof of everything.

The goal is not to make sampling complicated. It is to avoid paying for a sample that does not answer the decision you actually need to make.

## 2. Reddit Answer

Before ordering samples, I would ask what the sample is supposed to prove.

A lot of people only ask price + shipping, but the bigger issue is whether the sample is actually the same version you expect to order later.

I would ask the supplier:

- Is this a stock sample, similar sample, modified sample, or custom sample?
- What exact material, size, color, finish, and logo/customization will be included?
- Is the packaging the same as bulk packaging, or just sample packing?
- What is the sample lead time?
- What shipping method will be used?
- Can you break down sample cost, shipping cost, tooling/setup cost, and any refundable amount?
- What may change when we move from sample to bulk production?
- What needs to be reconfirmed before a mass order?

One thing I would be careful about: sample packaging and bulk packaging are often different. If packaging matters for your product, do not assume the sample packaging is what you will get in bulk.

A sample does not confirm everything about bulk production. It is just one checkpoint. Make the supplier write down what the sample proves and what it does not prove before you pay.

## 3. Quora Answer

Short answer: before ordering samples from a Chinese supplier, ask what the sample is meant to prove, which exact product version will be sent, and what may still change before bulk production.

Here are the main questions I would ask:

1. What type of sample is this?
Ask whether it is a stock sample, similar sample, modified sample, or fully custom sample. These are not the same thing.

2. What exact product version will be sent?
Confirm material, size, color, finish, logo position, customization, accessories, and any standard that matters to your order.

3. Is the packaging final?
Ask whether the sample packaging matches future bulk packaging or is only temporary sample packaging.

4. What is the full sample cost breakdown?
Separate product sample cost, shipping, tooling, setup fee, logo fee, and any refundable amount.

5. What is the sample lead time and shipping method?
Ask when the sample will be ready, which shipping method will be used, and who is responsible for customs or delivery issues.

6. What may change in bulk production?
Ask the supplier to list what may change later: material batch, color tolerance, packaging, mold, MOQ, lead time, unit price, or production process.

Example:
If a supplier sends a sample in plain packaging but your bulk order needs printed retail packaging, the sample does not confirm packaging quality, box structure, print color, or barcode placement. Those details need to be confirmed separately.

A sample is helpful, but it is not proof that the bulk order will be identical. It works best when both sides write down what the sample is meant to confirm.

## 5. Short FAQ Block

### 1. Is a sample enough to confirm bulk production quality?

No. A sample can confirm specific details such as appearance, material direction, size, finish, or function. It does not automatically confirm every bulk production detail.

### 2. What should I ask before paying for a sample?

Ask what type of sample it is, what exact version will be sent, what it is meant to prove, how much it costs, how it will ship, and what may change before bulk production.

### 3. Should packaging be confirmed at the sample stage?

Yes, especially if packaging affects retail presentation, protection, labeling, or compliance with buyer requirements. Ask whether sample packaging is the same as bulk packaging or only temporary.

### 4. What can change between sample and bulk production?

Material batch, color, finish, packaging, logo method, tooling, MOQ, lead time, unit price, and process details may change. Ask the supplier to list possible changes in writing.

### 5. What should be written in a sample order?

Write the sample purpose, exact product version, material, size, color, finish, packaging, customization, cost breakdown, lead time, shipping method, and what must be reconfirmed before mass order.
