## 1. LinkedIn Long Post

On a supplier video call, "show me the factory" is too broad.

The better request is:

Show me the areas connected to my order.

A live video call can reduce confusion because the buyer can ask follow-up questions in real time.

But the call is only useful if it focuses on specific details.

I would ask to see four things:

1. Sample room or current samples
Ask whether they can show samples similar to your product category, not just a general showroom.

2. Production flow related to your product
Ask which steps are done in-house and which steps are handled by outside partners.

3. Packaging area
If packaging matters, ask to see boxes, cartons, inserts, labels, or protective packing connected to similar products.

4. Customization and internal control
Ask who handles logo, tooling, finishing, inspection steps, and final packing inside the supplier's process.

A recorded factory video is better than nothing.

But live video is more useful because the buyer can ask:

- Can you move closer?
- Can you show the label?
- Is this product made here?
- Which step is outsourced?
- Is this sample current or old?

Video cannot prove everything.

But it can make vague supplier claims more concrete before the buyer moves forward.

## 2. Reddit Answer

On a video call, I would ask them to show specific areas related to your order, not just "the factory."

Things I would ask to see:

- sample room or similar products
- production area connected to your product category
- packaging area
- cartons, labels, inserts, or protective packing if packaging matters
- warehouse or finished goods area if relevant
- any machines or tools used for your product type

I would also ask questions while they are showing it:

- Which steps are done in-house?
- Which steps are outsourced?
- Who handles customization or logo work?
- Who checks defects before packing?
- Is this current production or just an old sample?
- Can you show something similar to the item you quoted?

Live video is usually more useful than a recorded video because you can ask them to move the camera, zoom in, or show another area.

But I would not treat a video call as proof of everything. It can reduce confusion, but it does not tell you whether the whole order will go well.

Also, showroom-only calls are weak. I would rather see a messy but relevant sample room or packaging area than a polished office.

## 3. Quora Answer

Short answer: on a Chinese factory video call, ask the supplier to show order-related areas and details, not only the office, gate, or showroom.

Here are the checks I would use:

1. Ask for live video when possible
A recorded video can be useful, but live video lets you ask follow-up questions, request close-ups, and compare answers with what you see.

2. Ask to see the sample room
Look for similar products, current samples, old samples, packaging examples, or materials connected to your product category.

3. Ask to see the relevant production flow
Ask which steps are done in-house and which steps are outsourced. The answer matters more than a broad "we make everything" claim.

4. Ask to see packaging and labels
If packaging matters, ask to see cartons, labels, inserts, retail boxes, barcode labels, or protective packing for similar products.

5. Ask who handles customization and checking
Ask who handles logo work, tooling, finishing, assembly, defect checks, and final packing.

6. Compare video details with the quotation
The video should make sense with the product they quoted. If the call shows only unrelated products or a showroom, ask more questions.

Example:
If you only see a showroom with finished products, you may learn what the supplier sells, but not much about production control. A sample room or packaging area connected to your product category is usually more useful.

A video call can reduce confusion, but it cannot prove everything about the supplier or the future order.

## 5. Short FAQ Block

### 1. Is a factory video call enough to verify a supplier?

No. A video call can show useful visible signals, but it cannot prove everything about the supplier or the future order. Use it to reduce confusion and decide what to ask next.

### 2. What should I ask a Chinese factory to show on video?

Ask to see areas related to your order: sample room, similar products, relevant production flow, packaging area, labels, cartons, warehouse, or machines connected to your product category.

### 3. Is a recorded factory video useful?

Yes, but live video is usually more useful. Live video lets you ask follow-up questions, request close-ups, and compare supplier answers with what is being shown.

### 4. Should I ask to see packaging and labels?

Yes, if packaging, labels, cartons, inserts, or retail presentation matter for your order. Packaging details often affect cost, lead time, and buyer expectations.

### 5. What can a video call not prove?

It cannot prove future production outcome, full capacity, compliance, or order performance. It only gives visible context and helps identify questions that need written confirmation.
