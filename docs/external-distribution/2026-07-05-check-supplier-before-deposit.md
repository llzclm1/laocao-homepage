### LinkedIn Long Post

Before paying a deposit to a Chinese supplier, the most important check is not whether the reply sounds professional.

It is whether the supplier has answered the decision-critical details clearly enough for you to move forward.

A smooth email can still leave important gaps.

I would slow down and check five things before wiring money:

1. Company match
Does the company name on the quotation match the invoice, bank account, website, and business documents?

2. Product match
Is the quoted product exactly the version you asked for, including material, size, finish, packaging, and any customization?

3. Payment account clarity
If the payment account name is different from the supplier name, is there a clear written explanation?

4. Sample and bulk assumptions
Is the sample a stock sample, similar sample, or custom sample? What may still change before bulk production?

5. Open questions
Has the supplier clearly written what is included, what is excluded, what is uncertain, and what still needs confirmation?

None of these checks prove that an order will go perfectly.

But they can reduce avoidable confusion before a buyer sends money.

The risk is often not one dramatic warning sign. It is a set of small missing details that only become expensive after payment.

### Reddit Answer

I would not treat a supplier as "checked" just because their reply looks professional or their price is good.

Before paying a deposit, I would ask for a written summary of a few basic things:

- exact product version being quoted
- material, size, finish, packaging, and customization details
- MOQ, lead time, sample terms, and what may still change before bulk
- invoice company name and payment account name
- what is included or excluded from the quote
- any assumptions the supplier is making

One practical thing: compare the supplier name across the quotation, invoice, email signature, website, and payment account. If the names do not match, that does not automatically mean it is bad, but I would ask them to explain it in writing before sending money.

Also ask whether the sample is a stock sample, similar sample, or custom sample. A lot of problems start because buyer and supplier both say "sample" but mean different things.

This will not prove the supplier is safe. It just helps you catch missing or vague information before you pay.

### Quora Answer

Short answer: before paying a deposit to a Chinese supplier, check whether the supplier has clearly confirmed the company, product, payment, sample, and order assumptions in writing.

Here are the main checks I would use:

1. Check the company names
Compare the quotation, invoice, bank account, email signature, website, and any company documents. If the names differ, ask for a written explanation before payment.

2. Check the exact product version
Make sure the supplier has confirmed the material, size, finish, packaging, customization, and any product standards that matter to your order.

3. Check the sample basis
Ask whether the sample is stock, similar, modified, or fully custom. Also ask what may still change before bulk production.

4. Check the quotation details
Do not only compare unit price. Check MOQ, lead time, tooling, freight terms, packaging, included items, excluded items, and payment terms.

5. Check vague replies
If the supplier avoids specific answers or rushes payment before clarifying details, slow down and ask for one written summary.

6. Check what is still unconfirmed
Ask the supplier directly: "What details are not final yet?" This often reveals assumptions that were not obvious in the original quote.

Example:
If the quotation says "custom packaging included" but does not say box size, print method, logo color, MOQ, or approval process, that is not clear enough before deposit.

These checks do not prove that a supplier is safe or that the order will be successful. They only reduce information gaps before you send money.

### Short FAQ Block

#### 1. Can I fully check a Chinese supplier before paying a deposit?

No. You can reduce information gaps by checking company names, payment details, quotation scope, sample terms, and product assumptions. These checks do not prove future performance.

#### 2. What is the first thing to check before sending a deposit?

Start with company and payment consistency. Compare the supplier name on the quotation, invoice, bank account, email signature, and website. If the names differ, ask for a written explanation.

#### 3. Is a professional supplier reply enough?

No. A professional reply can still miss key details like exact product version, packaging, sample basis, lead time, and excluded costs. Clear written answers matter more than polished wording.

#### 4. What should I ask about samples?

Ask whether the sample is stock, similar, modified, or custom. Also ask what the sample is meant to prove and what may still change before bulk production.

#### 5. What does a vague supplier answer mean?

A vague answer does not automatically mean the supplier is bad. It means you should slow down and ask for specific written confirmation before paying.
