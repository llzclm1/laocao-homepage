# Supplier Reply Review Case Library Batch 01

## Case 1

Case ID: SRR-CASE-001

Category: Payment

Buyer Situation:

A buyer is close to paying a deposit after receiving a quotation and proforma invoice. The supplier asks the buyer to send payment to an account that does not clearly match the company name used in earlier communication.

Purchase Stage:

Before payment

Material Received:

Supplier email, payment request, quotation, proforma invoice.

Supplier Statement:

"Please arrange payment to this account. It is our related account and can receive payment faster."

Visible Information:

- The product scope has been discussed.
- A quotation and payment request were provided.
- The payment account name is visible.
- The supplier gave a short explanation for the account difference.

Unclear Information:

- Whether the payment account belongs to the same legal entity as the invoice.
- Why the account name differs from earlier company information.
- Whether the invoice, payment account, and contract party should match.
- Whether the supplier can provide a revised invoice or written explanation.

Information Gap:

The buyer does not yet have a clear written connection between the supplier identity, invoice entity, and payment account.

Next Questions:

1. Can you confirm the legal company name that will issue the invoice?
2. Should the payment account name match the invoice company name?
3. If the account belongs to a related entity, can you explain the relationship in writing?
4. Can you send a revised proforma invoice showing the exact receiving account?
5. Who will be responsible for the order if payment is sent to this account?

Suggested Buyer Reply:

Hi, before we arrange payment, could you please confirm the legal company name for this order and the exact payment account name? We noticed the account name is different from the company name used in the quotation. Please also confirm in writing why this account is used and whether it is connected to the invoice company. Once this is clear, we can review the payment details internally.

Boundary Note:

This is not supplier verification, audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or payment safety advice. It only identifies communication gaps and questions the buyer may ask before making a payment decision.

## Case 2

Case ID: SRR-CASE-002

Category: Sample

Buyer Situation:

A buyer wants to order samples before placing a larger order. The supplier quotes a sample fee and says the fee can be refunded or credited later, but the terms are not specific.

Purchase Stage:

Sample

Material Received:

Supplier chat, sample quote, product link, shipping estimate.

Supplier Statement:

"Sample fee is refundable when you place bulk order. Please pay sample fee first and we will arrange soon."

Visible Information:

- The supplier has quoted a sample fee.
- The supplier mentioned a refund or credit.
- The buyer knows the sample is connected to a possible bulk order.
- Shipping may be separate from the product sample fee.

Unclear Information:

- Whether the sample is stock, similar, or customized.
- Whether shipping is included.
- Whether the sample fee is refunded as cash or credited against a future order.
- What order quantity is required for the refund or credit.
- Whether the sample will match bulk material, finish, logo, and packaging.

Information Gap:

The buyer does not yet know what the sample proves, what it includes, or how the refund/credit promise works.

Next Questions:

1. Is this a stock sample, similar sample, or custom sample?
2. Does the sample fee include shipping?
3. Will the sample fee be refunded or credited to a bulk order?
4. What order quantity is required for the refund or credit?
5. Which parts of the sample may change in bulk production?

Suggested Buyer Reply:

Thanks for the sample quote. Before we pay, could you please confirm whether this is a stock sample, similar sample, or custom sample? Please also confirm whether shipping is included and whether the sample fee will be refunded or credited to a future bulk order. If it is credited, please confirm the minimum order quantity required. We would also like to know which details of the sample will be the same in bulk production, including material, finish, logo, and packaging.

Boundary Note:

This is not supplier verification, audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or payment safety advice. It only clarifies what the supplier has and has not confirmed about the sample terms.

## Case 3

Case ID: SRR-CASE-003

Category: Quotation

Buyer Situation:

A buyer receives a quotation and later learns there may be an extra charge for logo printing or packaging. The buyer is unsure whether this is normal or whether the original quotation was incomplete.

Purchase Stage:

Quotation comparison

Material Received:

Supplier quotation, chat screenshots, product specification notes.

Supplier Statement:

"Logo printing has extra cost. The previous price is only for standard product."

Visible Information:

- The original quotation covered a standard product.
- The buyer discussed customization.
- The supplier now mentions an extra charge.
- MOQ and product quantity were part of the discussion.

Unclear Information:

- Whether the original quote included logo or packaging.
- Which logo method is being quoted.
- Whether there is a setup fee, per-unit fee, or both.
- Whether the charge changes at different quantities.
- Whether the updated quote changes lead time or sample requirements.

Information Gap:

The buyer cannot compare quotes fairly because the included and excluded items are not fully listed.

Next Questions:

1. Did the original quotation include logo printing or standard product only?
2. What logo method is included in the extra charge?
3. Is the extra charge a setup fee, per-unit fee, or both?
4. Does the charge change at different quantities?
5. Does logo printing affect sample time, bulk lead time, packaging, or defect allowance?

Suggested Buyer Reply:

Thank you for clarifying. To compare the quotation correctly, could you please send an updated quote showing the standard product price and the logo printing cost separately? Please include the logo method, any setup fee, per-unit fee, MOQ requirement, and whether logo printing changes sample time or bulk lead time. This will help us confirm the full scope before moving forward.

Boundary Note:

This is not supplier verification, audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or payment safety advice. It only reviews whether the quotation scope and extra charges are clearly communicated.

## Case 4

Case ID: SRR-CASE-004

Category: Factory Information

Buyer Situation:

A buyer asks for factory evidence before paying a deposit. The supplier sends a factory video showing machines, workers, and products, but the buyer is unsure what the video actually proves.

Purchase Stage:

Supplier selection

Material Received:

Factory video, product photos, supplier chat.

Supplier Statement:

"This is our factory video. We can produce this product, no problem."

Visible Information:

- A video shows a production environment.
- Machines, workers, or products may be visible.
- The supplier claims the video is connected to its factory.
- The supplier says it can produce the buyer's product.

Unclear Information:

- When the video was recorded.
- Whether the video shows the supplier's own facility.
- Whether the buyer's product or a similar product is shown.
- Whether any process is outsourced.
- Whether the sample and bulk order will be made in the same place.

Information Gap:

The video provides visible signals, but it does not confirm ownership, production control, order-specific capability, or sample-to-bulk consistency.

Next Questions:

1. When was this video recorded?
2. Does this video show your own factory or a partner facility?
3. Can you show a similar product currently in production?
4. Which steps are done in-house and which are outsourced?
5. Will the sample and bulk order be produced in the same workshop or process?

Suggested Buyer Reply:

Thanks for sharing the video. Could you please confirm when it was recorded and whether it shows your own factory or a partner facility? For our product, can you show a similar item currently in production or explain which steps are done in-house and which steps are outsourced? We are not asking for a formal audit, but we would like to understand how this video connects to our order.

Boundary Note:

This is not supplier verification, factory audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, payment safety advice, or a guarantee of order outcome. A factory video can show visible signals, but it cannot by itself confirm ownership, production control, or order outcome.

## Case 5

Case ID: SRR-CASE-005

Category: Factory Information

Buyer Situation:

A buyer asks whether a supplier can manufacture a customized product. The supplier replies broadly that it can make everything, but does not explain the production process, limitations, or required buyer materials.

Purchase Stage:

Research

Material Received:

Supplier reply, rough product description, reference images.

Supplier Statement:

"Yes, we can do everything. Please send order quantity and we quote best price."

Visible Information:

- The supplier is willing to quote.
- The buyer has shared a general product idea.
- The supplier has not rejected the customization request.
- Quantity is needed for pricing.

Unclear Information:

- Which parts of the product the supplier can make in-house.
- What details are needed before a meaningful quote.
- Whether tooling, sample development, or engineering review is required.
- What limitations or tolerances apply.
- Whether the supplier has made similar products before.

Information Gap:

The supplier's reply shows willingness, but not enough production-specific confirmation for the buyer to evaluate next steps.

Next Questions:

1. Which part of this product can you produce in-house?
2. Which parts, if any, would be outsourced?
3. What information do you need before quoting accurately?
4. Do you need drawings, dimensions, material specs, reference samples, or target quantity?
5. Have you made a similar product before, and can you describe the differences?

Suggested Buyer Reply:

Thanks for confirming. Before we discuss price, could you please help us understand what information you need for an accurate quotation? We can provide dimensions, material requirements, reference photos, target quantity, and packaging needs. Please also confirm which parts you can produce in-house and whether any steps would be outsourced. This will help us prepare the right materials before requesting a formal quote.

Boundary Note:

This is not supplier verification, audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or payment safety advice. It only reviews whether the supplier's capability communication is specific enough for the buyer to ask better next questions.
