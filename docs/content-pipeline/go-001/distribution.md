## LinkedIn Post Draft

A sample from a Chinese supplier is useful only when both sides agree what the sample is supposed to answer.

Before ordering, buyers should ask:

- Is this stock, custom, pre-production, or handmade?
- What exact material, color, size, finish, logo, label, and packaging are included?
- What is excluded compared with future bulk production?
- What are the sample fee, shipping cost, refund policy, and lead time?
- What details still need written approval before mass production?

The point is not to treat a sample as supplier verification. A sample does not prove factory reliability, final quality, or payment safety.

It is a communication clarity step.

If the supplier cannot explain what the sample will and will not prove, the buyer probably needs to slow down and ask better follow-up questions.

## Reddit Reply Draft

If you are ordering a sample from a supplier in China, I would not start with only "can you send a sample?"

Ask what the sample is supposed to prove.

The useful checklist is:

- exact product model/material/size/color/finish
- stock sample vs custom sample vs pre-production sample
- whether logo, label, packaging, manual, or accessories are included
- what is different from the future bulk order
- sample fee, tooling/printing fee, shipping cost, and refund policy
- sample lead time and shipping method
- whether they can send photos/videos before shipment
- what you still need to approve before mass production

Also keep the boundary clear: a sample is not supplier verification or a factory audit. It does not prove bulk quality or payment safety. It just helps you clarify whether the supplier understands the order well enough to continue the conversation.

If their answers stay vague or they push for payment before confirming details, I would slow down and ask for a written summary first.

## Quora Answer Draft

Before ordering a sample from a Chinese supplier, ask questions that clarify the sample scope, not just whether the supplier can ship one.

Key questions:

1. What exact product model, material, size, color, and finish will the sample use?
2. Is it a stock sample, custom sample, pre-production sample, or handmade sample?
3. Are logo, label, packaging, manual, or accessories included?
4. What will be different from the future bulk order?
5. What are the sample fee, tooling fee, printing fee, shipping cost, and refund policy?
6. How long will production and shipping take?
7. Can the supplier send sample photos or videos before shipment?
8. What details need written approval before mass production?

The important point is that a sample has limits. It can help you understand product details and communication quality, but it does not verify the supplier, audit the factory, guarantee final quality, or make payment safe.

Ask the supplier to write down what the sample will prove and what it will not prove. If they cannot answer that clearly, the sample may not answer the real sourcing question.
