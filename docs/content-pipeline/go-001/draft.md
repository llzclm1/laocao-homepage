# Questions Before Ordering Samples From China

Before ordering a sample from China, do not only ask, "Can you send a sample?"

Ask what the sample will prove, what it will not prove, and which details still need written confirmation before bulk production. A sample can be useful, but it can also create false confidence if the product scope, customization, packaging, fee, lead time, shipping, and next-step quotation are unclear.

This guide is about communication clarity. It is not supplier verification, a factory audit, quality inspection, payment protection, or a guarantee that a supplier is reliable.

## Direct Answer

Before ordering a sample from a Chinese supplier, ask these questions:

- What exact product model, material, size, color, and finish will the sample use?
- Is the sample a stock sample, custom sample, pre-production sample, or handmade sample?
- What customization, logo, label, packaging, or accessories are included?
- What is not included in the sample compared with future bulk production?
- What is the sample fee, mold fee, printing fee, shipping cost, and refund policy?
- How long will sample production and shipping take?
- Can the supplier send sample photos or videos before shipment?
- Will the bulk order use the same material, process, and production arrangement?
- What details need written approval before mass production?
- What will the next quotation be based on after sample review?

The goal is to make the supplier explain the sample scope clearly before you pay.

## Why Sample Questions Matter

Many sourcing problems start before the sample is even made.

A supplier may reply quickly and say they can send a sample, but the buyer may still not know:

- whether the sample matches the future order
- whether the sample is stock or customized
- whether packaging and branding are included
- whether the fee includes shipping
- whether the sample can be refunded after bulk order
- whether the sample proves anything about mass production

The useful question is not only "Can you send it?"

The useful question is: "What exactly will this sample answer, and what will still be unclear after I receive it?"

## Questions About Product Specification

Ask the supplier to confirm the exact product details in writing.

Useful questions:

- What is the exact model number or product name?
- What material will the sample use?
- What are the dimensions, weight, color, and finish?
- Are there any tolerances or acceptable variations?
- Does the sample match the product in the quotation?
- Are photos, drawings, or specification sheets available before production?

If the supplier answers only "yes" or "same as your request," ask them to repeat the exact details back to you. This helps reveal whether both sides are talking about the same product.

## Questions About Sample Type

Not all samples mean the same thing.

Ask:

- Is this a stock sample?
- Is this a custom sample?
- Is this a pre-production sample?
- Is this a handmade sample?
- Is this sample from previous production or made for my order?

A stock sample may show general product style, but it may not show your customization. A custom sample may show your requested details, but it may still not prove bulk production consistency. A pre-production sample may be closer to future production, but you still need to confirm what changes are allowed before mass production.

The sample type affects what you can learn from it.

## Questions About Customization And Packaging

If your order includes branding, packaging, labels, accessories, or special finishes, ask whether they are included in the sample.

Ask:

- Will the sample include my logo?
- Will it include final packaging?
- Will the color, surface finish, label, manual, or accessory be the same as the bulk order?
- Which parts are only for sample demonstration?
- Which parts will be different during mass production?
- Are there extra fees for logo, printing, mold, tooling, or packaging?

If packaging is not included, do not assume the sample shows the full customer-facing product.

## Questions About Fees, Lead Time, And Shipping

Before paying, ask the supplier to separate the costs.

Ask:

- What is the sample fee?
- Is there a separate mold, tooling, logo, or printing fee?
- Is shipping included?
- Which shipping method will be used?
- Who pays import duties or local delivery fees?
- Is any part of the sample fee refundable after a bulk order?
- How many days are needed to make the sample?
- How many days are needed for shipping?

If the supplier changes the fee, shipping method, or lead time after you ask for details, slow down and ask for a revised written summary before paying.

## What A Sample Still Does Not Prove

A sample can answer useful questions, but it has limits.

A sample does not prove:

- the supplier is verified
- the factory has been audited
- bulk production will be identical
- quality is guaranteed
- payment is safe
- the supplier is reliable
- the factory owns every production process
- future delivery will be on time

This does not mean samples are useless. It means buyers should treat samples as one communication step, not as final proof.

## Message Template To Send The Supplier

You can adapt this message before ordering:

```text
Hi,

Before I order the sample, please confirm the sample details in writing:

1. Exact product model, material, size, color, and finish.
2. Whether this is a stock sample, custom sample, or pre-production sample.
3. Which logo, label, packaging, manual, or accessory details are included.
4. Which details will be different from future bulk production.
5. Sample fee, mold/tooling fee, printing fee, and shipping cost.
6. Whether any fee is refundable after a bulk order.
7. Sample production time and shipping method.
8. Whether you can send photos or videos before shipment.
9. What details I need to approve before mass production.
10. What the next quotation will be based on after sample review.

Please also tell me what this sample can prove and what it cannot prove about the future bulk order.

Thanks.
```

## When To Slow Down

Slow down before paying if:

- the supplier only says "yes" without confirming exact details
- the sample type is unclear
- the supplier pushes for fast payment before answering scope questions
- sample fee, shipping cost, or lead time keeps changing
- logo, packaging, or customization details are not written down
- the supplier refuses to say what the sample does not include
- payment account details change or are not consistent with prior communication

These signals do not automatically prove a supplier is bad. They mean the communication is not clear enough yet.

## FAQ

### What should I ask before ordering a sample from a Chinese supplier?

Ask about product specification, sample type, customization, packaging, sample fee, shipping cost, lead time, refund policy, photos/videos before shipment, and what still needs approval before bulk production.

### Is a sample from China proof that bulk production will be the same?

No. A sample can show product details and communication quality, but it does not guarantee that bulk production will be identical. Ask what will be the same, what may change, and what needs written approval before mass production.

### Should I pay a sample fee before confirming product details?

You should first ask the supplier to confirm the product scope, sample type, fees, lead time, and shipping method in writing. Paying before these details are clear can create confusion later.

### What is the difference between a stock sample and a custom sample?

A stock sample is usually an existing sample that shows general product style. A custom sample is made or adjusted for your requested details. A pre-production sample is intended to be closer to the future bulk order, but it still does not guarantee final production results.

### Can I ask the supplier for sample photos or videos before shipment?

Yes. Photos or videos can help confirm visible details before shipment. They still cannot prove every material, process, quality, or production condition.

### What should I do if the sample fee or shipping cost changes?

Ask for a revised written summary that separates sample fee, tooling or printing fee, shipping cost, refund policy, and lead time. Do not rely on scattered chat messages if the amount has changed.

### When should I slow down before ordering a sample?

Slow down if the supplier avoids specific answers, changes fees repeatedly, pressures you to pay quickly, refuses to confirm details in writing, or cannot explain what the sample will and will not prove.
