Example 1

Payment Account Mismatch

Situation

The buyer is ready to pay a sample fee or deposit. The platform company name, invoice name, and bank account name are different.

Supplier Message

“Please send the deposit to this bank account. It is our finance account. The company name on Alibaba is different because we use another export company.”

What Is Confirmed

  • The supplier has requested payment.
  • The supplier says the bank account name is different.
  • The supplier says another export company is involved.

What Is Unclear

  • How the three company names are related.
  • Which company is responsible after payment.
  • Whether the PI can match the payment account name.

Questions To Ask Next

  • Can you explain the relationship between these company names?
  • Which company will be responsible for this order?
  • Can the PI show the same company name as the payment account?

Suggested Reply

Before I arrange payment, could you please clarify the relationship between the Alibaba company name, invoice company name, and bank account name? Please also confirm which company will be responsible for the order after payment.

Boundary Note

This does not prove the supplier is fake. It is an information gap before payment and should be clarified in writing.

Example 2

Supplier Quote Too Low

Situation

One supplier offers a price far below other quotes, but the explanation is vague.

Supplier Message

“Our price is much cheaper because we are factory direct. Quality is same, no problem. You can place order first and we can start quickly.”

What Is Confirmed

  • The quoted price is lower than other options.
  • The supplier claims to be factory direct.
  • The supplier wants the buyer to move quickly.

What Is Unclear

  • Which product version the price covers.
  • Whether material, packaging, logo, testing, or shipping is included.
  • Why the price is lower than the usual range.

Questions To Ask Next

  • What exact product version is included in this price?
  • What material, size, finish, packaging, and logo option does this quote include?
  • Are there extra costs not included in the unit price?

Suggested Reply

Thank you for the quotation. Before I compare it with other offers, could you confirm exactly what is included in this price, including material, size, finish, packaging, logo option, MOQ, lead time, and any extra costs?

Boundary Note

A low quote is not automatically a bad sign. The issue is whether the supplier can explain what is included and what may change later.

Example 3

Sample vs Bulk Difference

Situation

The supplier can send a sample, but the sample scope and bulk production assumptions are not defined.

Supplier Message

“Sample is available. You can check quality first. Bulk order will be no problem. Packaging can discuss after sample.”

What Is Confirmed

  • A sample is available.
  • The supplier expects the buyer to check it first.
  • Packaging has not been finalized.

What Is Unclear

  • Whether the sample is the exact future bulk version.
  • Whether material, color, logo, and packaging match bulk production.
  • Which details need reconfirmation before mass order.

Questions To Ask Next

  • Is this sample the exact same version as the future bulk order?
  • What material, color, finish, logo, and packaging will the sample include?
  • What may change between sample and mass production?

Suggested Reply

Before I order the sample, could you confirm what the sample will prove? Please confirm whether it will use the exact material, color, size, finish, logo, and packaging planned for the bulk order.

Boundary Note

A sample can reduce uncertainty, but it does not confirm every bulk production detail unless the sample scope is written clearly.

Example 4

Factory Video Visibility Issue

Situation

The supplier sends a recorded factory video, but it shows a showroom and general machines instead of order-relevant areas.

Supplier Message

“This is our factory video. You can see we are real factory. Many machines and workers. If you want order, please pay sample fee first.”

What Is Confirmed

  • The supplier provided a factory-related video.
  • The video may show a showroom, equipment, or workers.
  • The supplier wants the buyer to pay the sample fee.

What Is Unclear

  • Whether the video is current and order-relevant.
  • Which production steps are handled in-house.
  • Whether the supplier can show sample room or packaging area live.

Questions To Ask Next

  • Can we do a short live video call before sample payment?
  • Can you show similar products, sample room, and packaging area?
  • Which production steps are done in your own facility and which are outsourced?

Suggested Reply

Thank you for sending the video. Before I pay the sample fee, could we do a short live video call? I would like to see similar products, the sample room, packaging area if available, and the production steps related to this product category.

Boundary Note

Factory photos or videos are useful signals, but they are not formal audit evidence and cannot prove everything about the supplier or future order. See also Field Materials.

Example 5

Supplier Vague Technical Answers

Situation

The buyer asks about material, tolerance, finish, or customization. The supplier replies yes, but gives no numbers or specifications.

Supplier Message

“Yes, we can make it. Material is good quality. Size can be customized. Color no problem. We have done many orders before.”

What Is Confirmed

  • The supplier says customization is possible.
  • The supplier does not reject the technical request.
  • The supplier claims relevant experience.

What Is Unclear

  • Exact material grade.
  • Size range and tolerance.
  • Finish, color, logo method, MOQ, tooling cost, and lead time impact.

Questions To Ask Next

  • What exact material grade will you use?
  • What size tolerance can you control for this product?
  • Does this customization require tooling cost or higher MOQ?

Suggested Reply

Thanks for confirming that customization is possible. To avoid misunderstanding, please confirm the material grade, size tolerance, surface finish or color options, logo method, MOQ, tooling cost if any, and lead time impact.

Boundary Note

Vague technical replies do not prove the supplier is unreliable. They show that the buyer needs more specific written confirmation before sample payment or deposit.

FAQ

Are these real supplier cases?

No. These are anonymous example scenarios used to show how supplier communication gaps can be reviewed.

Can these examples prove whether a supplier is reliable?

No. They cannot prove supplier reliability, factory ownership, product quality, payment safety, or delivery outcome.

What do these examples help buyers understand?

They help buyers understand what a supplier has confirmed, what remains unclear, and what to ask next before sample fees, deposits, tooling money, or larger orders.
