How to Check a Chinese Supplier Before Payment

Use practical communication checks to review supplier reply clarity, information consistency, payment account details, and questions to ask before samples or deposits.

Short answer

Before paying a deposit, make sure the supplier, the product, the quote, and the payment account all line up in writing. A deposit is usually the moment when small unclear points become real risk.

Why this matters

Many buyers do some checking on the platform page, but they move to payment before the hard questions are fully answered. The problem is not only fraud. It is also mismatch. A supplier may be real, but the quoted product version, packing scope, sample basis, or payment company name may still be unclear. Once the deposit is sent, the buyer often feels committed and stops pushing for clarity.

The safer habit is simple: if a point would matter later in production, packing, shipping, or payment, clarify it before the deposit moves.

Step-by-step checklist

1. Confirm the legal company name shown on the business license, quotation, invoice, and payment account.

2. Confirm what exact product version is being quoted. Ask whether the quote is for a stock version, a similar sample, or your custom version.

3. Confirm materials, dimensions, packaging, labeling, and any accessories that are included or excluded.

4. Confirm MOQ, lead time, and whether those numbers are based on stock materials, standard packaging, or a real custom setup.

5. Confirm the sample path. Ask whether the sample is already available, needs to be remade, or still needs technical confirmation.

6. Confirm where the deposit will go and why that company or account name is used.

7. Keep the final assumptions in one written summary before payment.

Common red flags

One red flag is a payment account name that does not match the quoted supplier name, with no clear explanation. Another is when the supplier replies quickly but still does not define what product version the price is based on.

Be careful when the supplier avoids direct written answers on packaging, sample basis, or lead time assumptions. Be careful too when the price sounds good, but every important detail is still floating.

Example supplier questions

Can you confirm the legal company name that will appear on the invoice and payment account?

Is this quotation based on the exact product version we discussed, or on a similar existing model?

What packaging, labeling, and accessories are included in this price?

Is the sample stock, similar, or custom? What would still change before bulk production?

What information do you still need from us before the quotation becomes firm?

Supplier message

“Please pay 30% deposit so we can start production. Same material as sample. Lead time 20 days.”


What is still unclear

The exact sample version, material grade, packaging scope, lead-time start point, and beneficiary are not stated.


Useful response

Ask the supplier to confirm the product version, material, packaging, production start condition, payment account name, and what happens if the approved sample changes.

What to ask next before the deposit

Use these questions when a reply sounds positive but leaves the decision basis incomplete:

1. Which exact product and sample version does this deposit cover?

2. Which material, packaging, and labeling details are included in the quoted scope?

3. What event starts the 20-day production timeline?

4. Does the payment account name match the company or invoice details?

What this cannot prove

This process cannot prove supplier reliability, product quality, delivery outcome, legal standing, or payment safety. It only helps you make the visible communication and payment assumptions clearer before money moves.

FAQ

Can I fully confirm a Chinese supplier before paying a deposit?

No. You can reduce blind spots by checking company names, quotation details, sample terms, payment account names, and written assumptions, but this does not prove supplier reliability or payment safety.

What is the biggest risk before a deposit?

The biggest risk is paying before the product version, payment company, sample basis, packaging scope, and lead time assumptions are clear in writing.

Should the payment account match the supplier name?

The payment account should be explainable and consistent with the quotation and invoice. If names differ, ask for a written explanation before paying.

What should I ask before sending the deposit?

Ask what exact product version is quoted, what is included, what may still change, who receives payment, and what information the supplier still needs.

Should I pay a deposit before the sample is approved?

Do not treat a deposit request as a complete decision basis. Confirm what the deposit covers, which sample or specification it follows, and what approval or revision step comes first.

What if the supplier does not answer every question?

List the unanswered points in writing and ask for a direct response. Do not fill the gaps with assumptions about material, packaging, timing, or payment responsibility.

How do I know whether a quotation is for my exact product?

Match the quotation to the product version, material, dimensions, customization, packaging, MOQ, and sample basis discussed with the supplier.
