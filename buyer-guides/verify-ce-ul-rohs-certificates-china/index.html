Certificates

How to Verify CE, UL, or RoHS Certificates from a Chinese Supplier

Learn how to review CE, UL, RoHS, and other supplier certificates from China, including certificate scope, product model match, issuing body, expiry date, and common red flags.

Not a formal factory audit. Not legal due diligence. Not quality inspection. Not a supplier reliability guarantee.

Short answer

A certificate only matters if it matches the actual product, model, holder name, and order context you are discussing.

Why this matters

Certificates are easy to wave around and easy to misunderstand. Buyers often stop once they see a PDF, but the harder question is whether that document covers the exact model, product type, and company involved in the order. If it does not match, the document may still be real while still being irrelevant to your product.

Step-by-step checklist

1. Compare the product model and product scope with the quoted item.

2. Check the issuing body, holder name, and validity date.

3. Check whether the document is a product certificate, a test report, or a different file type.

4. Ask whether the quoted product version is exactly the one covered.

5. Keep the certificate review tied to the actual order context.

Common red flags

Red flags include expired files, unrelated model numbers, company names that do not line up, and generic test reports being presented as if they fully prove the quoted product. Another warning sign is when the supplier sends a certificate but avoids confirming whether the actual order version is covered.

Example supplier questions

Can you confirm the quoted product model matches the certificate scope?

Can you confirm the holder name and issuing body on this file?

Is this a product certificate or a test report?

Does this file cover the same material and version we are discussing?

What this cannot prove

This cannot prove ongoing production compliance, quality consistency, or shipment-level conformity. It only helps buyers review the visible certificate context more carefully.

Boundary

This guide helps review visible certificate information, not formal audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or supplier reliability guarantee.
