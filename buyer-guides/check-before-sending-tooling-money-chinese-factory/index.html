What to Check Before Sending Tooling Money to a Chinese Factory

Before sending tooling money to a Chinese factory, check ownership terms, drawings, sample approval, payment account, refund terms, timeline, and what happens if the project changes.

Not a formal factory audit. Not legal due diligence. Not quality inspection. Not a supplier reliability guarantee.

Short answer

Tooling money should only move once the buyer knows what is being built, who controls it, what approval path exists, and how changes are handled.

Why this matters

Tooling creates path dependency. Once the project is physically in motion, unclear ownership and vague scope become expensive.

Step-by-step checklist

Outline placeholder: drawings and version control, ownership terms, sample approval path, tooling timeline, payment name, and change/refund handling.

Common red flags

Outline placeholder: vague tooling scope, no written ownership language, unclear revision path, and pressure to pay before final drawings are aligned.

Example supplier questions

Outline placeholder: direct questions about tooling ownership, revision rounds, approval criteria, refund terms, and change management.

What this cannot prove

This cannot prove long-term project success, tooling performance, or dispute outcome.

Boundary

This guide helps reduce tooling-stage ambiguity, not formal audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or supplier reliability guarantee.
