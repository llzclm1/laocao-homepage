What Should I Ask a Chinese Supplier Before Ordering a Sample?

Before placing a sample order with a Chinese supplier, buyers usually need more than a product price. They need to understand what the sample includes, how much it costs, which specifications are confirmed, how long production and shipping may take, and which customization conditions apply.

This guide helps buyers organize questions and information before moving forward. It does not verify suppliers or guarantee sample or production results.

Prepared by Lao Cao from recurring supplier communication patterns. Examples are generalized and do not identify a customer or supplier.

Direct Answer

Before ordering a sample, ask the supplier to confirm:

  • Product specification
  • Sample type
  • Sample cost
  • Tooling or customization fees
  • Production time
  • Shipping method
  • MOQ
  • Payment details
Communication boundary

A sample order is one communication step. It can clarify product and commercial details, but it does not prove supplier reliability, factory capability, final production quality, or payment safety.

Why Sample Orders Need Clear Communication

A sample stage is not only about viewing or testing a product. It is also a way to clarify product requirements, understand how the supplier communicates, and document the proposed sample and production process.

The buyer should know what the sample can demonstrate, what it cannot demonstrate, and which conditions may change before bulk production.

Questions to Ask a Chinese Supplier Before Ordering a Sample

Use the following categories to organize the supplier's written confirmation.

1. Product Details

  • What exact specification is included?
  • Which material, dimensions, weight, colors, finishes, and functions are confirmed?
  • Does the sample match the version in the quotation?
  • Which details still require approval?

2. Sample Type

  • Is this a stock sample, reference sample, customized sample, or pre-production sample?
  • Which parts will be made for the buyer's requirements?
  • What may still change before bulk production?
  • Will the bulk order use the same material, components, labeling, and packaging?

3. Sample Cost

  • How much is the sample?
  • Does the cost include tooling, customization, or printing?
  • Is shipping included?
  • Is any part refundable or deductible from a bulk order?

4. Production Timeline

  • How long does sample production take?
  • When can shipping start?
  • Does the production timeline begin before or after payment?

Sample production time and delivery time are different. Confirm both separately.

5. MOQ and Bulk Order Conditions

  • What is the MOQ for the bulk order?
  • Will the sample specification affect MOQ or bulk pricing?
  • Which price tiers apply to different quantities?
  • Which sample conditions may change for bulk production?

6. Customization

  • Can size, material, color, logo, or packaging be customized?
  • Which options require tooling or development work?
  • Are there additional development costs?
  • What drawings, files, or references are required?

7. Payment Details

  • Who should receive the payment?
  • Which currency is used?
  • Which payment method and terms apply?
  • Can the supplier provide a written invoice or payment summary, and does the invoice name match the payment recipient?

8. Shipping and Packaging

  • Which shipping method will be used?
  • What is the estimated shipping cost?
  • What packaging is included?
  • Will sample and bulk packaging be different?
  • Can the supplier send current photos or a short video before dispatch?

Connect the Sample to Bulk Production

A sample only shows one unit at one stage. Before approving it, ask the supplier to identify which details are fixed for bulk production and which may still change.

  • Who records approved materials, dimensions, colors, finishes, labels, and packaging?
  • What happens if the sample has the wrong size, finish, color, or function?
  • Which adjustment must be completed before bulk production begins?
  • Will a revised sample or written approval be required after a change?
Do not assume “same as bulk”

Ask the supplier to state what will remain the same. A reference sample, a perfect sample, or pre-shipment photos do not by themselves confirm the result of a future bulk order.

Common Mistakes

  • Only comparing sample pricesA lower price may cover a different product scope, customization level, or shipping arrangement.
  • Asking only “How much?”The answer may not explain what is included or which terms apply.
  • Paying before confirming sample scopeConfirm the sample type, specification, fees, timeline, and shipping in writing.
  • Not confirming specificationsMaterial, dimensions, color, finish, logo, and packaging should not be left to assumption.
  • Confusing production and delivery timeSample preparation happens before shipping; carrier delivery begins afterward.

Key Takeaway

A sample order is not only a product test. It is also a communication process that helps buyers understand product details, sample terms, customization requirements, payment information, production timing, and the next steps before considering a bulk order.

FAQ

What should I ask a Chinese supplier before ordering a sample?

Ask the supplier to confirm the product specification, sample type, sample cost, tooling or customization fees, production time, shipping method, MOQ, payment details, and what may change before bulk production.

What is the difference between a stock sample and a custom sample?

A stock sample is an existing product that may show general design or quality. A custom sample is made or adjusted for the buyer's requirements. A pre-production sample should represent planned production more closely, but it still does not guarantee the final result.

Should sample production time and delivery time be confirmed separately?

Yes. Sample production time covers preparation before shipment. Delivery time begins after the sample has been handed to the carrier.

Does a sample guarantee that bulk production will be the same?

No. Ask what will remain the same, what may change, and what requires written approval before bulk production.
