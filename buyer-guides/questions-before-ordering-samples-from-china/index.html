Questions Before Ordering Samples From China

Before ordering samples from China, buyers should understand what the sample can prove, what it cannot prove, and what information still needs confirmation before bulk production.

Communication clarity only. Not supplier verification, factory audit, quality inspection, payment protection, or a supplier reliability guarantee.

Last updated: July 9, 2026

TL;DR / Direct Answer

Before ordering a sample from a Chinese supplier, ask what the sample will prove, what it will not prove, and which details still need written confirmation before bulk production.

Focus on product specification, sample type, customization, packaging, fees, lead time, shipping, refund policy, and next-step quotation basis.

Core boundary

A sample is one communication step. It can clarify information, but it does not prove supplier reliability, final quality, factory audit status, or payment safety.

Why sample questions matter

A supplier may reply quickly and say they can send a sample, but the buyer may still not know whether the sample matches the future order, whether it is stock or customized, whether packaging is included, or whether the fee includes shipping.

The useful question is not only "Can you send it?" The useful question is: "What exactly will this sample answer, and what will still be unclear after I receive it?"

Questions about product specification

Ask the supplier to confirm the exact product details in writing:

1. What is the exact model number or product name?

2. What material will the sample use?

3. What are the dimensions, weight, color, and finish?

4. Are there any tolerances or acceptable variations?

5. Does the sample match the product in the quotation?

6. Are photos, drawings, or specification sheets available before production?

If the supplier only says "yes" or "same as your request," ask them to repeat the details back to you. This helps reveal whether both sides are discussing the same product.

Questions about sample type

Not all samples mean the same thing. Ask whether it is a stock sample, custom sample, pre-production sample, handmade sample, or an old sample from previous production.

A stock sample may show general product style, but it may not show your customization. A custom sample may show requested details, but it still may not prove bulk production consistency. A pre-production sample may be closer to future production, but you still need to confirm what changes are allowed before mass production.

The sample type affects what you can learn from it.

Questions about customization and packaging

If the future order includes branding, packaging, labels, accessories, or special finishes, ask whether those details are included in the sample.

Ask whether the sample includes the logo, label, final packaging, manual, accessory set, color, surface finish, and any extra mold, tooling, printing, or packaging fee.

Also ask which parts are only for sample demonstration and which parts will be different during mass production. If packaging is not included, do not assume the sample shows the full customer-facing product.

Questions about fees, lead time, and shipping

Before payment, ask the supplier to separate sample fee, tooling fee, logo or printing fee, shipping cost, refund policy, sample production time, and shipping method.

If the supplier changes the fee, shipping method, or lead time after you ask for details, slow down and request a revised written summary. Treat the change as an information-consistency issue that needs clarification before the next step.

What a sample still does not prove

A sample can answer useful questions, but it has limits. It does not prove that the supplier is verified, the factory has been audited, bulk production will be identical, quality is guaranteed, payment is safe, the supplier is reliable, or future delivery will be on time.

This does not make samples useless. It means buyers should treat samples as one communication step, not final proof.

Useful signal

A clear supplier reply separates confirmed information, unclear information, missing details, and the next questions the buyer should ask.

Message template to send the supplier

Hi, before I order the sample, please confirm these sample details in writing:

1. Exact product model, material, size, color, and finish.

2. Whether this is a stock sample, custom sample, or pre-production sample.

3. Which logo, label, packaging, manual, or accessory details are included.

4. Which details will be different from future bulk production.

5. Sample fee, mold or tooling fee, printing fee, and shipping cost.

6. Whether any fee is refundable after a bulk order.

7. Sample production time and shipping method.

8. Whether you can send photos or videos before shipment.

9. What details I need to approve before mass production.

10. What the next quotation will be based on after sample review.

Please also tell me what this sample can prove and what it cannot prove about the future bulk order. Thanks.

When to slow down

Slow down if the supplier only says "yes" without confirming exact details, the sample type is unclear, fees or timing keep changing, customization details are not written down, or the supplier cannot explain what the sample will and will not prove.

These signals do not automatically prove a supplier is bad. They mean the information is not clear enough yet.

FAQ

What should I ask before ordering a sample from a Chinese supplier?

Ask about product specification, sample type, customization, packaging, sample fee, shipping cost, lead time, refund policy, photos or videos before shipment, and what still needs approval before bulk production.

Is a sample from China proof that bulk production will be the same?

No. A sample can show product details and communication quality, but it does not guarantee that bulk production will be identical. Ask what will be the same, what may change, and what needs written approval before mass production.

Should I pay a sample fee before confirming product details?

First ask the supplier to confirm the product scope, sample type, fees, lead time, and shipping method in writing. Paying before these details are clear can create confusion later.

What is the difference between a stock sample and a custom sample?

A stock sample is usually an existing sample that shows general product style. A custom sample is made or adjusted for your requested details. A pre-production sample is intended to be closer to the future bulk order, but it still does not guarantee final production results.

Can I ask the supplier for sample photos or videos before shipment?

Yes. Photos or videos can help confirm visible details before shipment. They still cannot prove every material, process, quality, or production condition.

What should I do if the sample fee or shipping cost changes?

Ask for a revised written summary that separates sample fee, tooling or printing fee, shipping cost, refund policy, and lead time. Do not rely on scattered chat messages if the amount has changed.

When should I slow down before ordering a sample?

Slow down if the supplier avoids specific answers, changes fees repeatedly, pressures you to pay quickly, refuses to confirm details in writing, or cannot explain what the sample will and will not prove.
