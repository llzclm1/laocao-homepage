Questions to Ask a Chinese Supplier Before Ordering Samples

Use this question list before ordering samples from a Chinese supplier, covering sample purpose, materials, packaging, customization, MOQ, lead time, and sample-to-bulk differences.

Short answer

Do not treat samples as a small shopping purchase. Treat them as the first chance to make the production assumptions visible.

Why this matters

Sample orders often go wrong because both sides think they are discussing the same thing when they are not. One side means a stock sample, the other means a true pre-production sample. One side assumes simple packaging, the other assumes retail packaging. The cost of the sample is usually smaller than the cost of misunderstanding what the sample is supposed to prove.

Step-by-step checklist

1. Ask what the sample is for: stock reference, similar sample, or custom sample.

2. Ask what materials, dimensions, color, finishing, and logo method the sample will actually use.

3. Ask what packaging version the sample will use and whether that packaging is representative of bulk production.

4. Ask what MOQ and lead time assumptions the supplier is using for later production.

5. Ask what parts of the sample may still change before bulk production.

6. Ask what is missing before the supplier can give a firm quote or firm production timeline.

Common red flags

A common red flag is when the supplier gives a sample price quickly but cannot explain whether the sample is stock, similar, or custom. Another is when the sample uses one material or packaging level while the bulk quote quietly assumes another. Be careful too when the supplier avoids putting the sample assumptions in writing.

Example supplier questions

Is this sample based on stock material, a similar model, or our custom specification?

What exact materials and finishing will be used for this sample?

Will the sample packaging match the planned bulk packaging?

What may still change between this sample and mass production?

What information do you still need before the quotation becomes firm?

What this cannot prove

This cannot prove future bulk consistency, quality control discipline, or shipment outcome. It only helps make the sample stage more specific.

FAQ

Is a sample always the same as bulk production?

No. A sample may be stock, similar, or custom. Ask what will still change before bulk production.

What should I confirm before paying for a sample?

Confirm sample purpose, materials, dimensions, finishing, packaging, customization level, lead time, and whether the sample supports a firm bulk quote.

Why do sample misunderstandings happen?

They happen when buyer and supplier use the word sample differently and do not write down what the sample is meant to prove.

Should I ask about bulk order terms before the sample?

Yes. MOQ, lead time, packaging, and customization assumptions should be discussed before ordering the sample.
