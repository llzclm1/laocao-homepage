What Documents Should a Chinese Supplier Provide Before an Order?

Learn what documents a Chinese supplier may provide before samples or orders, including business license, invoice details, product specs, certificates, packing information, and sample terms.

Not a formal factory audit. Not legal due diligence. Not quality inspection. Not a supplier reliability guarantee.

Short answer

Ask only for documents that help you make the supplier’s claims and assumptions more testable.

Why this matters

Buyers sometimes collect PDFs without knowing what they are checking. A better approach is to ask which document clarifies which risk. For example, a business license helps you compare entity names. A product spec sheet helps define what is being quoted. A sample term summary helps show what the sample is actually proving. The point is clarity, not paperwork volume.

Step-by-step checklist

1. Ask for the business license to compare legal entity names.

2. Ask for invoice or quotation details tied to the actual product version.

3. Ask for product specs, dimensions, materials, and packaging details.

4. Ask for certificate copies only when they are relevant to the product.

5. Ask for written sample terms and what still may change before bulk.

Common red flags

Watch for company names that shift across documents, certificates that do not match the product model, and quotations that still leave packaging or material scope unclear. Another red flag is when the supplier can send files quickly but cannot explain how those files relate to your actual order.

Example supplier questions

Can you send the business license for the invoicing company?

Can you confirm which product model this document refers to?

Can you send the current packaging and labeling specification?

Can you summarize the sample terms and what still changes before bulk?

What this cannot prove

Documents alone cannot prove product quality, factory reliability, or shipment outcome. They only help make the order context clearer.

Boundary

This guide helps identify practical documents, not formal audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or supplier reliability guarantee.
