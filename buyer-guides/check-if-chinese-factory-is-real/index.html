Factory Signals

How to Check If a Chinese Factory Is Real

A practical checklist for overseas buyers to check whether a Chinese supplier may be a real factory, including workshop evidence, product control, business license, video call, and reply consistency.

Not a formal factory audit. Not legal due diligence. Not quality inspection. Not a supplier reliability guarantee.

Last updated: July 5, 2026

Short answer

You usually cannot prove a factory is “real” from one signal. What you can do is check whether the business details, workshop evidence, and product-specific answers all point in the same direction.

Why this matters

Many buyers rely too much on profile photos, platform badges, or one short video. Those can be useful, but they do not tell you enough on their own. A supplier can have a real office or a real business registration and still be weak on your product category. The better question is not just “is this a company?” but “does this company seem to control the kind of product and process I care about?”

The goal is to reduce blind spots before samples, deposits, or tooling money.

Step-by-step checklist

1. Ask for the business license and compare the company name with the quotation and payment account.

2. Check whether the supplier’s visible product range makes sense. A very broad catalog can be normal, but it can also mean weak specialization.

3. Ask for workshop or production evidence that matches your product category, not just generic building shots.

4. Ask for a short video call or product-specific video. The best videos connect the product, the process, and the packaging flow.

5. Watch reply consistency. Real process familiarity usually shows up in the details of how a supplier answers technical and packing questions.

Common red flags

Red flags include generic workshop photos with no product link, highly polished profiles with weak technical answers, and refusal to show any process context during a video call. Another warning sign is when the supplier can talk generally about products but avoids direct answers about materials, dimensions, sample differences, or packing details.

Example supplier questions

Can you show the current workshop area related to this product type?

Can you walk through the sample room, packaging area, or current production area on a video call?

What parts of this product are done in-house and what parts are outsourced?

Can you show a recent production example that is close to this SKU?

What this cannot prove

This cannot prove legal standing, stable future capacity, quality systems, or shipment outcome. It only helps you read visible signs more carefully.

Boundary

This guide helps read visible evidence, not formal audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or supplier reliability guarantee.

FAQ

Can one video prove a Chinese factory is real?

No. A video can provide useful visible signals, but it cannot prove legal standing, future capacity, quality systems, or supplier reliability.

What factory signals should I ask for?

Ask for product-related workshop areas, current samples, packaging flow, process explanation, and answers that match the specific product you are buying.

Are platform badges enough to prove a factory?

No. Badges can be one signal, but they should be checked against business details, product-specific answers, workshop evidence, and payment information.

What if the supplier is a trading company?

A trading company is not automatically bad. The key is whether they can explain control, sourcing, production responsibility, and communication clearly.
