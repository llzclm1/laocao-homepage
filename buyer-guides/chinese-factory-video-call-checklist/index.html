Chinese Factory Video Call Checklist for Overseas Buyers

Use this Chinese factory video call checklist to ask for workshop, sample room, packaging area, labels, production flow, and product-specific evidence before placing an order.

Not a formal factory audit. Not legal due diligence. Not quality inspection. Not a supplier reliability guarantee.

Last updated: July 5, 2026

Short answer

A useful video call should show process and product relevance, not just a quick visual tour of a clean building.

Why this matters

Video calls are one of the easiest ways to reduce ambiguity when the buyer is remote. But they only help if the buyer asks to see the right things. Many calls stay too general. The camera moves through a hallway, a reception area, or one room, but the buyer still learns very little about the actual product flow.

The best video call is structured around your product, your packaging, and the real production path.

Step-by-step checklist

1. Ask for a short workshop route related to your product category.

2. Ask to see the sample room or recent product examples close to your SKU.

3. Ask to see the packaging area, labels, cartons, or barcode setup if packing matters.

4. Ask the supplier to explain which process steps are done in-house and which are outsourced.

5. Ask for one or two product-specific proof points instead of only general company introduction.

Common red flags

Be careful if the supplier only shows generic areas, avoids production flow, or keeps the camera fixed in one safe location. Another red flag is when the supplier describes strong production control but will not show packing, labels, current samples, or process continuity.

Example supplier questions

Can you show the workshop area linked to this product type?

Can you show a recent sample or recent production item close to this SKU?

Can you walk through the packaging and labeling area?

Which steps are done in-house and which steps are outsourced?

What this cannot prove

This cannot prove compliance, future quality consistency, sustained capacity, or full production control. It only helps buyers ask for more useful visible evidence.

Boundary

This guide helps structure a practical video call, not formal audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or supplier reliability guarantee.

FAQ

What should I ask to see on a factory video call?

Ask to see product-related workshop areas, sample room, packaging area, labels, recent examples, and process steps connected to your product.

Can a video call replace an audit?

No. A video call gives visible communication signals. It does not replace a formal audit, legal due diligence, or quality inspection.

What is a weak video call?

A weak call only shows reception areas, hallways, generic rooms, or fixed camera views without product-specific context.

Should I record the supplier answers?

Keep a written summary after the call, especially for in-house steps, outsourced steps, sample status, packaging, and open questions.
