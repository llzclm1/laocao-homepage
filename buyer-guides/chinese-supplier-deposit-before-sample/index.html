Deposits and samples

Chinese Supplier Asks for a Deposit Before a Sample: What Should You Confirm?

A deposit request before a sample can leave the buyer comparing payment pressure with incomplete product information. Use one written checklist to clarify what the payment covers, what the sample represents, and what still needs an answer.

This is communication guidance for buyers. It is not supplier verification, a factory audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, payment safety advice, or a guarantee of an order outcome.

Prepared from recurring supplier communication patterns. Examples are generalized and do not identify a customer or supplier.

Direct answer

Before paying, ask the supplier to separate the sample cost, any tooling or setup cost, shipping, and any amount that will later be credited toward production. A short request such as “Please pay first so we can make the sample” does not define the product basis or the next approval step.

Payment is not the same as clarity

The request confirms that the supplier wants payment before the sample step. It does not by itself confirm the sample specification, final quotation scope, or bulk production terms.

Break the reply into confirmed and open points

Use the supplier's own words, then mark what the message still leaves open.

Supplier saysWhat it confirmsWhat is still unclear
“Please pay a deposit before we make the sample.”A payment is requested before sample work begins.Sample type, amount allocation, product version, and approval step.
“The sample fee will be returned with the bulk order.”The supplier has described a possible credit.Which order qualifies, when the credit applies, and whether shipping or tooling is included.
“The sample can be ready in 7 days.”The supplier provided a time estimate.Whether seven days means production completion, dispatch, or arrival.

Six details to write down before payment

1. Sample basis. Ask whether the sample is stock, similar, custom, or a pre-production version. State the product version, material, dimensions, finish, and logo or artwork.

2. Payment allocation. Separate sample materials, tooling, artwork, setup, shipping, and any future-order credit. Ask for the amount and currency for each.

3. Approval path. Ask who approves the sample, how revisions are recorded, and which approved version will be used as the reference for a later order.

4. Timing. Ask what event starts production and what event starts shipping. Keep production time and delivery time as separate lines.

5. Payment recipient. Compare the company, invoice, quotation, and beneficiary names. If they differ, ask for a written explanation.

6. Change conditions. Ask which changes can alter the sample cost, MOQ, packaging, or lead time before you approve the next step.

Questions to send back

Keep the reply specific and easy to answer:

1. What exact product version and material will the sample use?

2. Which part of the payment covers the sample, tooling, artwork, shipping, or future-order credit?

3. Is the sample stock, similar, custom, or pre-production?

4. What does the stated production time begin from?

5. What changes are included before approval, and what would cost extra?

6. Which company name and payment account will appear on the invoice?

Ask for one written summary

If the answers arrive across several chats, combine the final product, payment, sample, and timing assumptions into one message before acting.

Anonymous example: deposit before sample

Generic example only. It does not refer to a real buyer, supplier, or order.

Supplier message

“Please pay $150 deposit. We will make the sample in 7 days. If you place a bulk order, we can return the sample fee.”


What is confirmed

The supplier requests $150 before sample work and gives a seven-day estimate.


What is missing

The sample type, material, shipping, whether tooling is included, what “return” means, and which event starts the seven days are not defined.


Next question

Could you confirm the sample specification, payment allocation, shipping method, approval process, credit conditions, and whether seven days means production completion or delivery?

FAQ

Should I pay a Chinese supplier deposit before seeing a sample?

Do not treat the request alone as a complete decision basis. Clarify what the deposit covers, what the sample represents, which product and packaging details are included, and what happens if the sample needs changes.

What should a deposit before a sample cover?

Ask whether the payment covers sample materials, tooling, artwork, production, shipping, or a later order deposit. Each item should be stated separately.

What should I ask when a supplier wants payment first?

Ask for the exact product version, sample scope, fee or deposit allocation, payment recipient, production start point, shipping method, and the conditions for changes or approval.

Does a deposit request prove that a supplier is unreliable?

No. It only shows that the supplier is requesting payment before the sample step. The buyer still needs clear written terms and may need independent commercial or legal advice.
