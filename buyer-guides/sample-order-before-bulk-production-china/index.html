Sample Orders

What Should Be Written in a Sample Order Before Bulk Production?

A sample order from China should clearly define product specs, materials, packaging, customization, lead time, shipping, sample limits, and what must be confirmed before bulk production.

Not a formal factory audit. Not legal due diligence. Not quality inspection. Not a supplier reliability guarantee.

Short answer

The sample order should remove guesswork. If key assumptions are still verbal or implied, the sample order is doing too little work.

Why this matters

Bulk disputes often start because the sample order never clearly defined what the sample was proving.

Step-by-step checklist

Outline placeholder: specs, materials, packaging, logo/customization method, sample quantity, shipping, lead time, and approval checkpoints.

Common red flags

Outline placeholder: missing version control, vague packaging, unclear logo method, no approval criteria, and no written link to future bulk production.

Example supplier questions

Outline placeholder: direct questions about sample version, what is excluded, what needs approval, and how bulk will match the sample.

What this cannot prove

This cannot prove future bulk consistency, shipping outcome, or final product quality.

Boundary

This guide helps write a clearer sample order, not formal audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or supplier reliability guarantee.
