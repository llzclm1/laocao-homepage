Sample Orders

What Should Be Written in a Sample Order Before Bulk Production?

A sample order from China should clearly define product specs, materials, packaging, customization, lead time, shipping, sample limits, and what must be confirmed before bulk production.

Not a formal factory audit. Not legal due diligence. Not quality inspection. Not a supplier reliability guarantee.

Short answer

The sample order should define what the sample represents, what it does not represent, and what must still be confirmed before bulk production.

Why this matters

Sample disputes often happen because the buyer assumes the sample is a miniature version of future bulk production, while the supplier treats it as a partial reference. If the sample order does not define materials, packaging, logo method, and approval criteria clearly, both sides may leave with different expectations.

Step-by-step checklist

1. Define the exact product version and drawing basis.

2. Define materials, finishing, colors, and customization method.

3. Define what packaging version the sample uses.

4. Define sample quantity, shipping method, and lead time.

5. Define what must be approved before bulk production starts.

Common red flags

One red flag is when the sample order only lists a short product name and a price. Another is when packaging, labeling, or logo method are left open. If the supplier cannot say what still may change between sample and bulk, the sample order is still too vague.

Example supplier questions

Can you confirm what exact spec version this sample follows?

Can you confirm which details are representative of bulk and which are not?

What approvals do you need from us before bulk starts?

What still may change after the sample stage?

What this cannot prove

This cannot prove future bulk consistency, shipping outcome, or final product quality. It only helps define the sample stage more clearly.

Boundary

This guide helps write a clearer sample order, not formal audit, legal due diligence, quality inspection, or supplier reliability guarantee.
